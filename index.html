Understanding Deductive and Simple Interest

In financial mathematics, interest represents the cost of using borrowed money or the return on deposited capital over time. While ordinary simple interest is paid at the conclusion of a loan term, deductive interest (frequently termed interest in advance or bank discount) is deducted upfront at the moment funds are released.

Key Distinction: When borrowing ₱10,000 under ordinary simple interest, you receive ₱10,000 on day one and pay back the principal plus interest at maturity. Under deductive interest, the interest is subtracted immediately from your loan proceeds.

Net Proceeds = P - I

Foundational Concepts

How Mathematical Interest Operates

Whenever capital is borrowed or invested, the party utilizing the money pays a rental fee known as interest. In college business mathematics, the two most common elementary structures of simple interest are Ordinary (Add-On) Simple Interest and Deductive Interest (Discount in Advance).

Feature Ordinary Simple Interest (Add-on) Deductive Interest (In Advance)
When Interest is Paid Paid at the end of the term (maturity date). Deducted immediately on day 1 (drawdown date).
Cash Received by Borrower Full Face Value / Principal (\(P\)). Net Proceeds (\(P - I\)), which is less than Face Value.
Repayment at Maturity Principal plus Interest: \(A = P + I\). Face Value (\(P\)), since interest was already paid upfront.
Common Use Cases Bank savings accounts, time deposits, personal loans. Promissory notes, commercial paper, salary advances, microloans.
Effective Interest Rate (EIR) Equal to the nominal contractual rate (\(r\)). Higher than the nominal rate, because you hold less cash.

Essential Mathematical Terminology

Before performing any calculations, students must master the precise definitions of each financial parameter:

Principal (Face Value) P

The initial capital invested, deposited, or the stated face value of a loan note in Philippine pesos (₱).

Annual Interest Rate r

The percentage charged or earned per annum. It must always be converted into a decimal fraction before computation (e.g., \(5\% = 0.05\)).

Time or Term t

The length of the loan or deposit expressed strictly in years. If given in months, divide by 12 (e.g., 6 months = 0.5 years).

Interest Amount I

The quantitative peso fee earned by the lender or paid by the borrower for the use of the principal.

Total Maturity Amount A

The cumulative balance at the end of the term in ordinary interest, equal to the principal plus accumulated interest (\(P + I\)).

Net Proceeds Proceeds

In deductive interest loans, the actual cash handed to the borrower on day one after upfront interest is deducted (\(P - I\)).

Why Deductive Interest Matters to Filipino Consumers

Under Republic Act No. 3765 (The Philippine Truth in Lending Act), creditors must issue a full Disclosure Statement before a contract is signed. Because deducting interest upfront means you receive less than the face amount, the Effective Interest Rate (EIR) is always higher than advertised. College students must know how to uncover the true cost of credit.

Mathematical Reference

Formulas and Algebraic Derivations

All calculations for simple interest and deductive interest stem from linear algebra. Below is the complete mathematical toolkit required for college examinations.

Core Calculation

1. Simple Interest Formula

Calculates the monetary interest charged or earned over a duration \(t\) on principal \(P\).

I = P × r × t
I = Interest in Philippine pesos (₱)
P = Principal or face value (₱)
r = Annual rate as decimal (e.g., 0.04)
t = Duration in years (\(\text{months} / 12\))

Application Condition: Use when interest does not compound periodically and rate is given on a yearly (per annum) basis.

Maturity Value

2. Total Amount (Maturity Value)

Calculates the total balance accumulated in a savings account or owed on an ordinary loan at maturity.

A = P + I = P(1 + rt)
A = Total future amount (₱)
P = Original principal (₱)
(1 + rt) = Linear growth multiplier

Algebraic Derivation: Substituting \(I = Prt\) into \(A = P + I\) yields \(A = P + Prt\). Factoring out \(P\) produces \(A = P(1 + rt)\).

Deductive Method

3. Deductive Loan Proceeds & Effective Rate

Calculates the upfront interest deduction, the cash proceeds released to the borrower, and the true effective rate.

Proceeds = P - I = P(1 - rt)
reff = I ÷ (Proceeds × t)
Proceeds = Net cash received on day 1 (₱)
P = Face value repaid at maturity (₱)
reff = True Effective Annual Interest Rate (EIR)

Key Rule: Because the borrower only utilizes the Proceeds while paying interest calculated on the full Principal, \(r_{\text{eff}}\) is strictly greater than nominal \(r\).

Algebraic Rearrangements

4. Formulas for Unknown Variables

When interest is known but one of the other parameters is missing, rearrange \(I = Prt\):

P = I ÷ (r × t)
r = I ÷ (P × t)
t = I ÷ (P × r)

Time Check: Remember that solving for \(t\) always gives the time in years. To convert into months, multiply \(t\) by 12.

Example 1: Maria's Savings Deposit

Mandatory College Benchmark
Problem Statement: Maria deposits ₱5,000 into a savings account that earns 4% simple interest per year for 2 years. How much interest will she earn, and what will her total balance be after two years?
Step 1
Identify the given values:
Principal: P = ₱5,000
Annual interest rate: r = 4% = 0.04
Time: t = 2 years
Step 2
Select the formula:
I = Prt
Step 3
Substitute the values:
I = 5,000 × 0.04 × 2
Step 4
Calculate the interest:
I = ₱400
Step 5
Calculate the total amount:
A = P + I
A = ₱5,000 + ₱400 = ₱5,400

Final Answer & Interpretation

Maria earns ₱400 in simple interest. Her total balance after two years is ₱5,400, assuming no additional deposits, withdrawals, or administrative fees.

Example 2: Deductive Loan with Upfront Discount

Deductive Method
Problem Statement: Angelo borrows ₱20,000 from a financing company for 1 year at an 8% deductive interest rate (interest collected in advance). Compute: (a) the upfront interest deduction, (b) the net proceeds Angelo receives on day one, (c) his repayment at maturity, and (d) the true effective annual interest rate.
Step 1
Identify the given parameters:
Face Value (Principal): P = ₱20,000
Contract Rate: r = 8% = 0.08
Term: t = 1 year
Step 2
Calculate interest deducted in advance (\(I\)):
I = Prt = 20,000 × 0.08 × 1 = ₱1,600
Step 3
Calculate the net cash proceeds delivered to Angelo:
Proceeds = P - I = ₱20,000 - ₱1,600 = ₱18,400
Step 4
Determine repayment at maturity:
Because interest was already taken upfront, Angelo pays the face value of ₱20,000 at the end of the year.
Step 5
Calculate the true Effective Interest Rate (EIR):
Angelo paid ₱1,600 to hold and use only ₱18,400 for 1 year:
reff = I ÷ (Proceeds × t) = 1,600 ÷ (18,400 × 1) ≈ 0.086957 = 8.70%

Final Answer & Interpretation

Angelo receives ₱18,400 at drawdown and repays ₱20,000 at maturity. While the nominal contract stated 8%, Angelo's true borrowing rate was 8.70%.

Example 3: Time Deposit with Duration in Months

Unit Conversion
Problem Statement: Juan places ₱25,000 in a rural bank special time deposit earning 3.6% simple interest per year for 9 months. What is the total interest earned and the final maturity amount?
Step 1
Convert time to years:
Since 1 year = 12 months, t = 9 ÷ 12 = 0.75 years.
Rate: r = 3.6% = 0.036. Principal: P = ₱25,000.
Step 2
Calculate simple interest:
I = Prt = 25,000 × 0.036 × 0.75 = ₱675.00
Step 3
Calculate the maturity value:
A = P + I = ₱25,000 + ₱675 = ₱25,675.00

Final Answer & Interpretation

Juan earns ₱675.00 in interest. At the end of 9 months, he withdraws a total balance of ₱25,675.00.

Error Prevention

Frequent Student Misconceptions

Carefully analyze these five recurring traps observed in college business mathematics exams.

Trap 1: Rate Input

Using Percent Instead of Decimal

Plugging \(r = 5\) instead of \(r = 0.05\) inflates the computed interest by a factor of 100.

Correction: Always divide the percentage by 100: \(r = 5\% = 5 / 100 = 0.05\).
Trap 2: Time Unit Mismatch

Plugging Months Directly into t

Using \(t = 9\) for a 9-month note mistakenly calculates interest for 9 full years!

Correction: Divide months by 12: \(t = 9 / 12 = 0.75\text{ years}\).
Trap 3: Terminology Confusion

Confusing Interest with Total Balance

Assuming the answer to "How much will Maria have?" is the interest (\(I = ₱400\)) rather than the total balance (\(A = ₱5,400\)).

Correction: Check whether the prompt asks for interest earned (\(I\)) or maturity value (\(A = P + I\)).
Trap 4: Deductive Cash Flow

Assuming Face Value is Disbursed

Believing that on a ₱50,000 deductive loan, the borrower gets ₱50,000 cash on day one.

Correction: Interest is taken upfront; the borrower receives only the net proceeds (\(P - I\)).
Trap 5: Nominal Rate Illusion

Treating Contract Rate as Effective Cost

Assuming an 8% deductive loan costs the same as an 8% ordinary simple interest loan.

Correction: Upfront deduction shrinks available principal, making the true effective borrowing rate (\(r_{\text{eff}}\)) higher.
Philippine Context

Real-World Financial Applications

Simple interest and deductive discounting are applied every day across Philippine banking, consumer credit, and small business operations.

Commercial Banking

Philippine Time Deposits

Major banks (such as BDO, BPI, and LandBank) offer 30-day, 90-day, or 1-year special time deposit accounts where capital is locked in at an agreed simple interest rate, paid upon maturity.

Consumer Lending

Salary Advances & Deductive Loans

When employees take a ₱20,000 emergency loan from credit unions or private lenders, the interest and doc stamps are often deducted upfront. The borrower receives ₱18,000 but repays the full ₱20,000 through payroll deductions.

SME & Microfinance

Sari-Sari Store Financing

Micro-entrepreneurs borrowing inventory capital must calculate upfront deductions to verify whether the net cash delivered will actually cover their supplier purchases.

Legal Protection

Truth in Lending Act (RA 3765)

Mandates that Philippine lenders provide full disclosure statements showing the gross principal, advance deductions, net proceeds, and true Effective Interest Rate (EIR) to prevent hidden fees.

Pre-Exam Review

Summary & Key Takeaways

  • Core Linear Formula: Simple Interest is governed by \(I = Prt\), where \(r\) is in decimals and \(t\) is in years.
  • Ordinary Total Balance: \(A = P + I = P(1 + rt)\) represents the final accumulated sum when interest is paid at the end.
  • Deductive Interest (In Advance): The lender calculates \(I = Prt\) and deducts it upfront. The borrower receives \(\text{Proceeds} = P - I\) on day one and repays \(P\) at maturity.
  • True Effective Borrowing Cost: Because deductive loans provide less than face value in cash, the true Effective Interest Rate is \(r_{\text{eff}} = \frac{I}{\text{Proceeds} \times t}\).
  • Time Precision: Always convert months to years (\(t = \text{months} / 12\)) before substituting into the formulas.
